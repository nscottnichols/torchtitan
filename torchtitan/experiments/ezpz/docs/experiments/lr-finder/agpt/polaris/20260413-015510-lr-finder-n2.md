## Method

Exponential LR sweep following [Smith 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01186).
At each step, the LR is multiplied by `mult = (max_lr / init_lr)^(1/N)` = 1.148.
Loss is tracked with EMA smoothing (beta=0.98, bias-corrected).

The **optimal LR** is estimated as the blow-up point divided by 10,
or equivalently the LR at the steepest descent phase.

The LR finder curves are highly consistent across A100 (Polaris) and Intel Max 1550

the hardware. Minor differences in absolute loss values reflect different hardware
numerics (bf16 implementations).

## Key Findings

### 1. Optimizer sensitivity ordering (confirmed)

Same as Aurora — for 2B the optimizers rank by LR sensitivity:

```
AdamW (most tolerant) > Muon > SophiaG (most sensitive)
```

### 2. Cross-hardware consistency

Blow-up LRs and optimal LRs agree within ~1.5x across A100 and Intel Max 1550:
- AdamW blow-up: ~3e-2 (both)
- Muon blow-up: ~1.5e-2 (Polaris) vs ~1e-2 (Aurora)
- SophiaG blow-up: ~4e-3 (both)

### 3. SophiaG catastrophic divergence (confirmed)

SophiaG diverges to NaN on Polaris (vs loss=294.6 on Aurora), confirming the
Hessian-based preconditioning produces unstable updates at high LRs.

### 4. 20B AdamW reproduces Aurora

The 20B AdamW sweep completed on a second allocation (4951s). Results match
Aurora closely: min loss 11.38 @ LR 3.5e-3 (Aurora: 11.43 @ 4.0e-3), blow-up
at ~8e-3 (Aurora: ~5e-3), final loss 85.7 (Aurora: 57.1). The slightly higher
blow-up LR on Polaris may reflect A100 vs Intel Max numerical differences.
